# Short paper - 1

## Title

- AI Agent-Orchestrated Mobility Simulation: Scenario Generation and System Performance Evaluation

### **1. Introduction**

- **1.1 Background and Motivation**
    - Challenges in configuring and operating transport simulations
    - The potential of LLMs to simplify simulation control via natural language
- **1.2 Objectives**
    - Propose and implement an AI agent-based architecture for simulation automation
    - Demonstrate feasibility with real-world data and use cases
    - Lay the foundation for future multi-agent systems
- **1.3 Contributions**
    - Design and implementation of a LangChain-based simulation control framework
    - Integration with a modular transportation simulator
    - Experimental validation with urban taxi data
    - Conceptual roadmap toward multi-agent orchestration
- **1.4 Paper Organization**

---

### **2. Related Work**

- **2.1 Transportation Simulation Systems**
    - Overview of existing simulators (e.g., SUMO, MATSim)
- **2.2 AI Agents and LLM Integration**
    - Use of LLMs in software control and simulation environments
    - Examples of LangChain, ReAct, AutoGPT, and agent frameworks
- **2.3 Natural Language Interfaces for Simulation**
    - Voice/chat-based system control interfaces in prior studies
- **2.4 Research Gap and Positioning**
    - Most existing simulation systems require manual or script-based configuration, which can be cumbersome and inflexible.
    - Prior studies have integrated LLMs into software workflows, but have rarely applied them to simulation orchestration in a modular, extensible way.
    - There is limited research on using **AI agents to dynamically control simulation execution via natural language**, especially in the **transportation domain**.
    - While some multi-agent LLM frameworks exist (e.g., for planning or debate), **no prior work has applied multi-agent interaction to transportation simulation setup or policy evaluation.**
    - This paper does **not yet implement a multi-agent architecture**, but proposes a validated single-agent framework and **outlines a roadmap toward multi-agent coordination** in future research.
