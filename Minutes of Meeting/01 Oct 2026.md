## **1\. Research paper and models**

* The paper uses three approaches to predict bus arrival time:  
  * **Historical average:** estimates travel time from previous journeys.  
  * **Kalman filter:** adjusts estimates using current conditions, including bus speed.  
  * **ANN predictor:** uses time of day (**T**), source stop (**I**), and destination stop (**J**). It was described as the most accurate approach in the paper.  
* The **first task is to reproduce the paper**. The team should check whether its steps, code, and GitHub repository are available, then implement and test the original models. Priya has already prepared pseudocode.  
* After reproduction, the team will define hypotheses, explore the paper’s suggested improvements, and add its own contributions. A **transformer** was discussed as a possible improvement, but the available data may be too limited. An **LLM or agent-based model** was also discussed; hallucination was raised as a concern. The suggested comparison starts with basic ANN and deep-net baselines, then tests transformers and possibly LLMs. These are options to investigate, not completed model choices.

## **2\. Vishal’s dwell-time analysis**

* Vishal showed a way to separate **dwell time**—time spent at a stop for boarding and offboarding—from time spent travelling between stops.  
* His proposed method places a **30-metre boundary** around each stop: 15 metres before and 15 metres after it. A bus inside that boundary is treated as dwelling; between stops, it is treated as travelling.  
* In the test Vishal presented, the reported prediction error changed from **29.5 seconds without dwell time to 25.2 seconds with it**, which he described as about **14% better**. This was a result of the test shown in the meeting, not a general performance claim.

## **3\. Data needed for better predictions**

* The next recommendation was to divide each bus route into **smaller road segments**. Segments may differ in traffic, road layout, turns, intersections, and signals, so each may take a different amount of time to cross.  
* The team should prepare **segment-wise travel or traffic data in five-minute intervals**, with the day and time attached. Historical data could then help estimate travel time for a particular segment at a particular time—for example, morning versus evening traffic.  
* Data preparation and useful features were emphasized as central to prediction quality. Passenger boarding and offboarding counts would also help estimate dwell time, if available. The discussion noted that tap-on data alone may not reveal where passengers get off. A 2026 paper on bus demand prediction was mentioned as a reference to look up.
